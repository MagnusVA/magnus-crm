Call insights: what prospects say on Fathom calls

For every Fathom recording matched to a sales call, Claude reads the full transcript and pulls out the prospect's objections, struggles, goals and stated income. Each item is checked against the transcript before it is saved. The results show on the person's profile and in a new section of Reports → Calls.

What you get

On every person's profile

  • The objections they raised, on which call, and whether each was resolved on that call.
  • Their struggles and goals, grouped into fixed categories, with the exact sentence they said.
  • Every income figure they stated: personal income, business revenue, profit, savings or budget, kept separate. Each has its currency, period and date, and a target is never mixed with current income.
  • One click from any item to the moment in the recording.

In Reports → Calls

  • How often each objection comes up, by closer, program and period.
  • How often objections are resolved on the call.
  • Close rate by objection, and close rate by income band, on the same first-held-call basis as the Scorecard.
  • The top struggles and goals over time, and how many calls were read.
Cost per call
≈ $0.06

Sonnet 5.5 on a 45-minute call. Haiku 5.5 is about $0.003 if it passes the same quality test.

Monthly cost
≈ $20

About 300 recordings a month (306 meetings had a Fathom link in October). Capped at $40 by default.

History it can read
90 days

Fathom transcripts are deleted after 90 days. The backlog starts about 4 October, so the first transcripts expire around 2 January, then about 10 a day.

It uses the same Convex AI Gateway that Jev already calls, through its Anthropic Messages endpoint with zero data retention. No Anthropic key and no new vendor are needed. Jev keeps doing its job, the second opinion on what the closer recorded, and nothing about it changes.

How it works

A recording that matches a meeting already triggers the second opinion. It will also queue one extraction, with its own queue, budget and records.

Green boxes are new, grey boxes exist today, blue boxes are where the data is read. Red marks the private table: transcript quotes and statements stay in Convex and never reach ClickHouse.

One run, start to finish

A cap that is reached keeps the run queued with a later start time, so it continues next month or after the owner raises the cap. A worker that dies after claiming is picked up by a recovery sweep, and its reserved cost stays charged until it settles.

Data model

Everything is additive: new tables and one optional tenant field. Nothing on leads changes and nothing is copied onto the person. The person is always worked out through recording → meeting → deal → person, the same way callRecordings works today. That means a merge, a deal move or a reassignment needs no repair.

Every matched recording has one header row in callInsightFacts (kind = call), whether or not it has been read. The header holds the call's status and reason (read, waiting, failed, skipped), the main blocker, and which run is published. ClickHouse can therefore compute coverage and failure reasons for past days without ever seeing the private runs.

Each rebuild of a call's rows bumps publicationRevision: a publish, a rematch, or a repair. Reports count an item only when its revision and meeting match the header's, and only when the number of items equals expectedItemCount. A Fivetran sync that lands halfway through a rebuild therefore shows the call as "syncing" instead of a mix of two versions.

The header is created when the call is first matched and is never deleted. "Not a sales call" only marks it inactive, so its revision counter and its link to the published run survive a later rematch. ClickHouse sees one stable row per call, not a deleted row and a new one.

The header also records which person the "is this the right prospect" check ran against, and that person's identityVersion at the time. identityVersion is a new counter on leads that goes up whenever the person's email or email identifiers change: a merge, an edit, a new identifier. If either value no longer matches, readers show the call as "rechecking" until the check runs again, which the same change schedules straight away. ClickHouse checks the person only, because a new column on leads reaches the warehouse late. The re-check finishes long before the next 6-hourly sync anyway.

Extraction

The request

POST https://ai-gateway.convex.dev/v1/messages
Authorization: Bearer <getServiceToken("ai-gateway")>

{
  "model": "anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5",
  "max_tokens": 8000,
  "system": "<fixed, versioned rules + taxonomy>",
  "messages": [{ "role": "user", "content":
    "#1 [00:00] C: Hey Marcus, can you hear me?\n
     #2 [00:04] P: Yeah, all good…\n …" }],
  "output_config": {
    "format": { "type": "json_schema", "schema": … },
    "effort": "low"
  },
  "provider": { "zdr": true,
    "data_collection": "deny",
    "require_parameters": true }
}

No temperature (Claude 5.5 models reject it) and no forced tool choice (Sonnet 5.5 rejects it with a 400). The JSON schema sent to the model has its length and count limits removed, because structured output doesn't accept them. Code enforces those limits after the response.

What goes in, what doesn't

  • In: the full transcript as numbered lines, with each speaker labelled Closer, Prospect or Other by the existing labelSpeakers.
  • Out: Fathom's summary (another model's reading, which can't be checked line by line), the deal stage, the outcome and the closer's record. None of these should colour what the prospect is heard saying.
  • Long calls: the whole transcript is always sent. A 3-hour call is about 36k tokens. Above 150k tokens the run fails visibly as "too long" instead of quietly cutting the middle. The inventory step measures real lengths first.
  • Short calls: no 100-word minimum. One sentence can hold an income figure. The only skip is a call where the prospect never speaks.
  • Injection: the system prompt treats the transcript as data. The output can only be categories and short strings, and every item has to quote a real line.

What code checks before anything is saved

Speaker labels can be wrong. Fathom often has no email for the prospect, so labelSpeakers usually works by elimination: whoever isn't the closer is the prospect. That is fine when only one other person is on the call. When two or more people other than the closer end up labelled Prospect, such as a partner who joined, or when no one is labelled Closer at all, the run is marked speaker uncertain. Its items are kept with a warning, but its income figures stay out of the income bands. The call is not thrown away: calls where a partner joins are exactly the ones a partner-objection report needs. Income is also dropped unless the amount appears in the verified quote ("six K", "6,000"). That rule is the code-level guard against invented income. The check understands Spanish too ("cinco mil", "lucas").

Income

Income is a list of statements, not one number. In the same call a prospect can give their salary, what their business bills, what they take home and what they could put in. Treating any of these as "income" would make the band report wrong.

Rules

  • The model returns the numbers as text. Code converts them to minor units using a table of how many decimals each currency has. Zero is allowed.
  • Ranges stay ranges. A range that crosses a band boundary goes in a "spans bands" row instead of being split at its midpoint.
  • Monthly figures are worked out only for week, month and year amounts, and are labelled as worked out.
  • Currencies are never added together or converted. Bands are per income type and per currency.
  • Income from closer lines, hypothetical examples and uncertain speakers is never counted.

  • Every rule is enforced in Convex queries, not only by hiding UI. New permissions: call-insights:manage (owner) and call-insights:view (owner, admin).
  • Prospect quotes are deleted when Fathom's transcript is (day 90), on every run, including the older purge path. A run that finishes after that date can't bring a quote back. Short English statements, categories and income stay, just as Fathom's summary does today.
  • Only the text-free facts table is ever enabled in Fivetran. Runs, budgets and eval cases stay in Convex. Before deploy, check that the connector is still on "Allow columns" so new tables arrive blocked.
  • The eval stores no copies of model output. Its runs are marked purpose: eval: they never publish, never take a live run number, and lose their quotes on the same day as live runs. Labels are item verdicts and line numbers, with no text.
  • When the switch is off, the report panels return nothing, cached answers included, so a rollback really hides them.
  • Logs and PostHog get IDs, statuses, counts, tokens and cost. They never get quotes, statements or income figures.
  • The model is reached only through ZDR endpoints. If a request can't be routed to one, it fails rather than falling back to another provider.

Rollout on the live tenant

  1. 0
    Pre-deploy checks

    The Fivetran connector is on "Allow columns", so new tables arrive blocked. The deployment's AI Gateway spend limit has room above Jev's limit plus both call-insight limits, because reaching it disables the whole deployment.

  2. 1
    Deploy with the switch off

    New tables and an optional tenant field only. No data migration. Existing behaviour is unchanged, and the Jev and Fathom test suites must pass as they are.

  3. 2
    Smoke test on dev

    A made-up transcript through the gateway on both models confirms that structured output passes through with ZDR, and shows the usage fields and latency.

  4. 3
    Inventory, with no model calls

    Matched recordings, transcript lengths, languages, expiry dates, estimated cost, and how often the speaker labels are guessed, show two prospects or show no closer. This decides whether long-call splitting is needed at all. A one-off migration also creates the status header for every recording already matched, including expired ones, before Fivetran sees the table.

  5. 4
    Evaluation on about 40 real calls

    The calls are chosen to cover long calls, several income figures, no income, a partner present, and any Spanish calls. Admins accept or reject each extracted item next to its transcript line, in a small review panel in the recording dialog. That takes minutes per call. One admin reads about 20 calls in full to check what was missed and the income figures, and a second settles only the disagreements. The eval runs inside production and stores only verdicts.

  6. 5
    Shadow

    The eval picks the model first, so the backlog is read once with the model that will stay. Each transcript can only be read before it expires. The owner then switches to shadow. New calls are read as they arrive, and the backfill runs soonest-to-expire first under its own limit (the estimate plus 30%). The backfill preview suggests that month's overall limit (live calls, plus backfill, plus eval), which may be above the $40 default. The backfill must finish before the first transcripts expire around 2 January. Admins see the cards with a "Preview" badge.

  7. 6
    Review

    Go through the failures, coverage and agreement with Jev. Rerun anything that needs it.

  8. 7
    Warehouse

    Enable the facts table in Fivetran, wait for the first sync, apply the 0004 view, deploy the report definitions, and run the parity check.

  9. 8
    On

    The owner switches it on: reports appear and the Preview badge goes. To roll back, switch it off. That stops new runs and hides the readers, and the data is kept.

Testing

Automated

  • Contract: real quotes, paraphrases, wrong lines and injected quotes; closer lines dropped; resolution downgraded without prospect evidence.
  • Income: every period, ranges, zero, unknown currency, spans bands, several statements in one call.
  • Gateway: 200, refusal, cut off, 400, 429 with Retry-After, 502/503 and malformed responses, all mocked.
  • Lifecycle (convex-test): a duplicate webhook gives one run; a throwing hook doesn't undo Fathom ingest; two runs racing at the limit are both checked against reservations; month boundary; a stale worker can't publish; a failed rerun keeps the old facts; ten failed reruns still show the last good one.
  • Moves: "not a sales call" removes the facts; a rematch moves them without a model call and with a new revision; a merge or deal move is correct in the person page and in reports right away, before any nightly job; partial syncs after a rerun, a rematch from A to B, back from B to A, and an ignore all show as "syncing".
  • Budget: backfill runs racing at both limits; a duplicate settlement counts once; a recovered attempt keeps its origin.
  • Eval isolation: an eval that finishes after the live run, followed by a rematch, still publishes the live run; an eval started during a live run doesn't block it; a backfill of an eval call still queues one live run.
  • Identity: right after a merge that adds an email, or an email edit, the call shows "rechecking" or the re-checked result, before any cron.
  • Lost hook: if the queueing step rolls back completely, the 15-minute sweep over matched recordings finds the call, creates its header and queues it, and does so only once even when it races the normal trigger. The sweep only queues calls matched after the feature was turned on: switching on with 50 older calls queues none of them, and the capped backfill picks them up.
  • Retention: both purge paths, and a run that finishes after expiry.
  • Access: owner and admin can read; closer, lead gen and other tenants are refused.
  • Reports: distinct-call counting, zero-objection calls in the denominator, first-call basis, per-currency bands, partial-sync guard, parity and merge tests.

Quality bar before "on"

  • No invented income. Zero tolerance.
  • Income fields (type, current or target, amount, period, currency) ≥ 90% correct, on at least 30 income statements.
  • Objection precision ≥ 90%, recall ≥ 80%.
  • Resolved or unresolved ≥ 85% correct.

Measured on about 40 labelled calls for both models. The cheapest model that passes becomes the default. You can change these thresholds.

After launch

  • Agreement with Jev on the main objection, as a drift signal, not ground truth.
  • Dropped-item rate and failure reasons per prompt version.
  • A small random monthly audit, so checks don't only look at flagged calls.

Phase 2: where this can go

Not in the first release. All of it builds on the same runs and facts.

Qualification

Who decides (alone, with a partner, approver on the call), how soon they need to decide, and whether a next step was agreed and then kept.

Closers

Closers see insights on their own meetings, plus a pre-call brief built from earlier calls: "last time: partner objection, wants out of the gym by summer".

Assistant

The MCP magnus_get_recording returns insights, marked as said by the prospect so the assistant treats them as data.

Segments

Filter people by objection or income band on the People report, and build respark lists from them.

Coaching

For each closer: objections left unresolved, how they were handled, and which ones carry over to the next call.

More sources

Any transcript in storage (Zoom, phone) feeds the same contract. Plus human corrections and a taxonomy each tenant can edit.

Reports

A labelled toggle that treats "currency not stated" as USD when reading, and bands for other currencies as they appear.

Older calls

Read calls older than 90 days by fetching their transcripts from Fathom again, held in memory only, if you allow it.

Follow-up calls

"Deals won after any call with this objection", alongside the first-call close rate.

How this plan was made

Five planners drafted on their own, each from a different angle: smallest change, correctness, verification, data and performance, and a different architecture. Two researchers checked the gateway and the sales-call taxonomy. Five new reviewers scored the drafts and raised blocking objections. The merged plan then went through three rounds of voting by five more reviewers, three Codex Sol and two Opus, with the plan fixed after each round.

  • The recovery sweep only queues calls matched after the feature was turned on. Older calls go through the capped backfill.
  • ClickHouse checks a call's person by ID only. The identity-version check stays in Convex, because the new column reaches the warehouse late.
  • Coverage splits held meetings only. "Matched, not held" is a separate count outside the percentages.

Decisions for you

  1. Taxonomy. Confirm or edit the objection, struggle and goal lists above for your programs.
  2. Who sees income. Owners and admins only in v1 (recommended), or closers for their own calls as well?
  3. Quotes after 90 days. Delete them with the transcript (recommended), or keep short quotes?
  4. Monthly limit. $40 a month, plus a one-off backfill limit of about $25 (the estimate plus 30%), or other amounts?
  5. Quality bar. Accept the thresholds above, and name the two admins who will review the eval calls (about 40 quick checks and 20 full reads).
  6. Older calls. Fathom's API can return transcripts older than 90 days. Should the backfill fetch them in memory only, or stick to the 90-day rule?
