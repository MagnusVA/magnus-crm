Reports, rebuilt

The seven report pages count calls, cash and people seven different ways. They cover one of the four roles that earn credit on a deal, and every number is a dead end. Nothing opens and nothing exports.

This is an audit of what the pages say today and of the signals the system already collects. It ends with mockups of six pages that share one dictionary, cover every hand on a deal, open any number down to the person behind it and export what you see.

01 In short

Three problems, one plan

The numbers drift

The same word does different math on different pages. Revenue leaves deposits out and the metrics API keeps them in. "Deals" counts payment rows. Booked counts a moved call twice. The Revenue page adds dollars and euros and prints the sum as dollars. Filters narrow one card and leave the rest of the page alone.

The people are missing

Team Performance lists active phone closers and nobody else. Setters have a separate Slack report. DM closers and lead generators only show up on Overview and Operations. Nobody can open one person and see what they did last month.

The plan

One PR. One dictionary that every page, help line and export reads, Overview and Operations included. Six report pages instead of seven: Scorecard, Calls, People, Attribution, Pipeline and Log, with a member report behind every name. Every range except a single day reads ClickHouse with today live on top, and every page exports CSV, Excel and PDF from the answer it shows.

Where Overview ends and Reports begin

Overview stays what it is: the floor right now, with today's rankings and the phone closers. Operations stays the place where work gets done: booking reviews, no-shows to chase, qualifications to read. Reports answer a different question. What happened over a period, who did it, and how much money did it make? Anything that is a period total or one person's history belongs in Reports. Anything that tells someone what to do next stays where it is. Both adopt the same dictionary in the same PR, so a word like Canceled or Cash means one thing on every page.

Every Reports page follows three rules that today's pages break. A filter scopes the whole page or it isn't in the band. Every name, deal and call on the page opens. Every figure names its grain and its timestamp, in the card and in the export.

03 Why the numbers drift

Nineteen reasons two pages disagree

Each finding comes from the code on main and names the file. Red changes a number someone reads. Amber makes a correct number easy to misread. Grey is something missing.

Who gets counted

Team means active phone closers

Wrong scope

getActiveClosers returns users with the closer role and isActive. Setters, DM closers and lead generators never appear. A closer who left mid-month drops out of revenue while their deals stay in the close-rate cohort.

convex/reporting/lib/helpers.ts:48

Cash credited to nobody disappears

Silent gap

Revenue by closer lists active closers only, so the rows add up to less than the page total. Sales Calls has the same gap and says so in its definitions card. The report pages don't say it.

convex/reporting/revenue.ts:114 · convex/reporting/teamPerformance.ts:347

Conversions only count for active closers

Silent gap

A first enrollment whose closer has left counts toward time to convert but not toward Conversions or the closer table.

convex/reporting/leadConversion.ts:170

A new closer inherits the old one's calls

Wrong person

A follow-up booked on another closer's calendar, a reschedule to another host, or a sick-day redistribution sets every meeting on the deal to the new closer, past ones included. The first closer's held calls and no-shows move to the second, along with the deal's place in their close rate, whenever it happens.

convex/lib/syncOpportunityMeetingsAssignedCloser.ts:5 · convex/analytics/definitions/closeRateCohort.ts:22

What a word means

Revenue adds currencies together

Wrong number

The revenue readers sum amountMinor across currencies and the page prints the total in dollars. €8,000 and $8,000 become $16,000. Sales Calls keeps one total per currency.

app/workspace/reports/revenue/_components/revenue-format.ts:3

"Cash" means two things

Two totals

The metrics API's cash_collected includes deposits. Sales Calls' Cash collected leaves them out. Same name, different total, and an export built on either would disagree with the other.

convex/analytics/api/metrics.ts:283 · operations/sales-calls/_components/definitions-card.tsx

"Deals" are payment rows

Misleading

Revenue's Deals and Team's Payment sales count payments. A split plan paid in three installments is three deals, and Average deal divides cash by installments.

convex/reporting/revenue.ts:149

Lost deals counts meetings

Misleading

Team's Lost deals comes from the meeting outcome mix: meetings whose deal was lost by a person on that meeting. It isn't the number of deals that closed lost in the range.

convex/reporting/teamOutcomes.ts:223

Conversion rate can pass 100%

Bad ratio

It divides first enrollments in the range by people who reached Qualified in the range. They are different people, so the ratio has no meaning and a slow month of new leads can push it past 100%.

convex/reporting/leadConversion.ts:189

When something counts

A moved call is a cancellation

Wrong number

A Calendly reschedule sets the old meeting to canceled and books a new one. Booked counts both, Canceled counts the old one, and the outcome mix checks canceled before rescheduled, so Rebook rate almost never counts a real reschedule.

convex/pipeline/inviteeCreated.ts:1365 · convex/reporting/lib/outcomeDerivation.ts:79

A moved first call becomes a follow-up

Wrong number

A new meeting is a follow-up when its deal already has any meeting. After a reschedule it does, so the new slot for a first call is filed under Follow-up calls and first-call show rate loses it.

convex/pipeline/inviteeCreated.ts:164

Booked means "on the calendar"

Misleading

Booked counts meetings by when the call happens, not when someone booked it. A call booked on October 30 for November 3 is a November booking. Nothing counts bookings made in a range.

convex/reporting/teamPerformance.ts:225

Show rate can hide unrecorded calls

Misleading

Show rate is held ÷ (held + no-show), which is the right rule. A closer who leaves calls unrecorded keeps a clean rate, though. The proof line mentions recorded results; no column or ranking uses them.

convex/reporting/teamPerformance.ts:465

What a filter touches

Filters scope one card

Misleading

Team's filters narrow Phone closer operations only. Pipeline's narrow attendance only. Reminders' narrow money only. Activity's narrow the feed only. The band sits above all of it and looks filtered.

team-report-page-client.tsx:150 · convex/reporting/remindersReporting.ts:109

Tabs that don't scope their columns

Misleading

New calls and Follow-up calls switch the attendance columns and show the same close rate, cash and average sale in both tabs, which reads as if follow-up calls earned the same money.

app/workspace/reports/team/_components/closer-performance-card.tsx:49

Distinct counts double across the split

Wrong on split

When This month merges ClickHouse history with live today, Slack's unique deals and Reminders' chains add the two answers. A deal with events on both sides counts twice. The merge comments say so.

convex/analytics/merge/slackQualifications.ts:18 · merge/reminders.ts:51

07 One word, one formula

The dictionary every page and export reads

Each figure, its help line and the Definitions sheet in every export come from one module, lib/reporting/definitions.ts. Each term names its grain, meaning what one unit is, and the timestamp that puts it in a range. The red line under a card says what the word means on today's pages.

Calls scheduled

Grain · call

Calls whose time falls in the range, without the slots that were moved.

Calls scheduled=meetings in range−moved slots
range by meetings.scheduledAt

Today: Booked counts every meeting row, so a moved call counts twice.

Bookings made

Grain · call

Calls someone booked during the range, whenever the call is. A meeting that replaces another, a move or a rebook after a no-show, doesn't count again. DM closers' bookings use the same rule.

Bookings made=meetings created in range−replacements(rescheduledFromMeetingId set)
range by meetings.createdAt

Today: not counted on Reports. DM bookings count "anything but a follow-up", which ties them to the call type.

Follow-up call

Grain · call

A call on a deal where the person has already held one. Everything before that is a first call, moves and rebooks after a no-show included.

Follow-up=a held call on the same deal is scheduled before it
stored as callClassification, re-checked when a result is recorded

Today: any call on a deal that already had a meeting, so most follow-ups are moved first calls.

Rebooked after a no-show

Grain · call

A call booked to replace one the person missed, through the no-show link. It's a first call if the missed one was.

Rebooked=meetings whose replaced meeting is a no-show

08 Scorecard

The one big report: the whole period, first touch to cash

The Scorecard replaces Revenue, Leads & Conversions and Reminders, and takes the team totals from Slack Qualifications. It reads left to right in the order money is made: people captured, qualified, booked, held, won, paid. Each card below goes one level deeper and opens the list behind it. Try the presets and the Export menu; the funnel toggles between activity and a cohort.

09 Calls

Every booked call accounted for

Calls takes attendance out of Team Performance and Pipeline Health and makes it add up. Every slot on the calendar lands in exactly one bucket: moved, canceled, upcoming, held, no-show or not recorded. Show rate stays held ÷ (held + no-show), and it never appears without Recorded beside it.

10 People and member report

Everyone who works a deal, and one click into their month

People replaces Team Performance. It lists every team member from teamMembers, active or not, with a tab per role and an Everyone tab that puts them side by side on credited cash. Each role gets the columns that fit its work: attendance and sales for phone closers, qualifications and book rate for setters, bookings and portal work for DM closers, captures and resparks for lead gen.

Click any row. Admins open anyone's report; closers and lead generators open their own. The member report opens in place with that person's period: their figures beside the team's, every day of the range, the calls or captures they worked, the deals they touched and a log of everything they did. The same report opens from a name anywhere in Reports.

11 Attribution

Who is responsible for how much of the cash

The ADR settled that credit is a dealAttributions row with a role and a source, and that there are no commission shares. This page reads those rows against the cash collected in the range. Each role is a lane that adds up to the month's cash: the people who held that role on the paid deals, plus the cash nobody in that role touched.

Lanes don't add to each other. A $9,500 deal found by lead gen, qualified by a setter, booked by a DM closer and closed by a closer shows $9,500 in all four lanes. That is the honest answer to "who is responsible for this money". Splitting it would be commission math, which the ADR keeps out.

12 Pipeline and Log

Right now, and everything that happened

Pipeline Health mixed a range with the present. The rebuilt Pipeline has no range at all: it says "Live · right now" and lists what is owed, with every row opening the deal or call. The range parts of Pipeline Health move to Calls. Activity Feed becomes Log: one filter row that scopes the counts and the rows alike, a member filter, and an export of every row.

13 Ranges and export

Every range except a single day reads ClickHouse

The rule is short. Today, and a custom range of one day, read Convex live. Any range of two days or more reads ClickHouse. When the range includes today, the days before today come from ClickHouse and today comes from Convex, merged on the page, so today is never six hours old. This is the plan dashboardRangePlan already makes, with one addition: until a sync that ended after the business day started has landed, yesterday reads live as well, so a call logged late last night is never missing in the morning. Pick a preset to see which days come from where.

Why

Decided (D1). Ranges that include today stay split, with today live. The overnight addition costs one more cached answer a night and about a day of work in the planner, which needs the last sync time to decide.

The export button

Every report page gets Export in its band, beside the picker, in the band-button style Operations already uses. It exports what the page shows: the same range, the same filters and the same answer, never a fresh scan that could disagree with the screen. Each file is stamped with the range, the business-day rule, the filters and the as-of time.

The export reuses what Operations built: the CSV encoder in lib/operations-reports/csv.ts (UTF-8 BOM, CRLF, formula-injection guard), xlsx-js-style for workbooks, pdfkit for PDFs and Convex file storage for the download. Files are named magnus_<page>_<from>_<to>, with the member's name added on a member report. Amounts in two currencies are never added: each row carries its currency, and each total is per currency. Setters and DM closers can't sign in, so an admin sends them their member report as a PDF.

14 Underneath

What has to change under the pages

Most of the work is in the readers and ClickHouse definitions, not the UI. The engine in convex/analytics/ already does the hard parts: cached answers per sync, a pool that stays inside magnus_app's four queries, merges with tests, and parity checks against Convex. Ten pieces change.

1. One dictionary

lib/reporting/definitions.ts holds each term's label, grain, anchor timestamp, formula text and help line. Report pages, Overview, Operations, help tips, export Definitions sheets and the metrics API's descriptions all read it. Cash includes deposits, as the API's cash_collected already does, and Final cash sits beside it.

2. One rule for call types

A call is a first call until the person has held one on the deal: follow-up means "a held call on this deal is scheduled before this one". One function applies it in the three booking inserts in inviteeCreated.ts, in moveMeetingToOpportunity, and in patchMeetingLifecycle whenever a meeting's status moves to or from held, which re-types the deal's later calls. A backfill fixes the past, with a ledger so it can be undone exactly.

3. DM bookings stop depending on call type

Booked Calls, its export, the Overview leaderboard and the ClickHouse operations template count a DM booking as "anything but a follow-up". A rescheduled first call becomes a first call under the new rule, so it would count twice. A DM booking becomes "a meeting that doesn't replace another", whatever its type, which keeps DM goals where they are.

4. Moved slots

A canceled meeting that another meeting replaces is moved. Convex finds the successor through a new index on rescheduledFromMeetingId; ClickHouse finds it with a self-join. The Operations meeting projection gains a moved status, the reschedule path re-projects the slot it replaced, and deleting a successor re-projects it back. Overview, Sales Calls and Calls all show Canceled and Moved apart.

5. The closer at the time

A new host on a follow-up, a reschedule to another host and a sick-day redistribution all set every meeting on the deal to the new closer, past ones included, because access follows that field. Reports instead take each meeting's closer from its latest closer row in dealAttributions for that meeting, so held calls, no-shows, the close-rate cohort and time to record stay with the closer who had them. Reads only.

6. Credit

Credited cash for lead gen, setters and DM closers is the cash on deals where the person has a credit row, counted once per person per deal, in full for each person. Rows can add up to more than the lane, which shows the overlap as Shared. Closer cash is the payment's attributedCloserId. It's a record of who contributed, never of who is owed.

7. ClickHouse for every multi-day range

About twenty report definitions, each with a Convex reader for today and single days, a SQL template, a merge and the parity and merge tests from "Adding a report". Distinct counts carry IDs so the merge drops duplicates. The new page ids join analyticsPageValidator; switch:disable drops a page back to its Convex readers, which answer short ranges fully and mark long ones partial.

8. ClickHouse views

Not needed after all. The new reports read the existing views (0001 to 0003), so the PR has no ClickHouse migration. The schedule history, reassignments, notes and booking-review views stay a follow-up for when a report needs them.

9. Member identity and access

People lists teamMembers, inactive ones included, and attaches role records through teamMemberId. Nothing joins by name. Admins open anyone's member report. Closers and lead generators open their own through a new permission, and every query behind the report checks that the member is the viewer's teamMemberId.

10. Results owed

Every show rate sits beside Recorded and the show-rate floor, Calls lists the calls still owed a result, and the member report shows time to record. Nothing blocks or nags yet. The rules are in place for when recording is enforced.

Not in this PR

Later, by decision

Enforcing results

Recording a result for every call becomes required when the full rollout comes. This PR puts the rules and the visibility in place, with Recorded, the floor and the owed list, and doesn't block or remind anyone.

After the two-week watch

Narrowing

Aggregate components whose last reader was an old page, the old page ids in analyticsPageValidator, and the ledger. The PR description lists each one. Unmounting an aggregate can only be undone with a backfill, so each waits for the watch, as docs/plans/clickhouse-reporting.md §12.1 asks.

Left out on purpose

Commission and conversion

Credit records who contributed, never who is owed. Currencies stay apart; converting needs a rate table nobody maintains today.

Unchanged

Access to deals

Meetings still move to a deal's new closer, since what a closer can open depends on it. Only reports read the closer at the time.
