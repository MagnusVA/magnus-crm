How a person moves through the pipeline

Five channels find or create the same person record. Deals, meetings and payments then push that person along six lifecycle stages, which only move forward on their own. The map below shows where each channel's people get on and how far they get.

    The cast

    Four records, each with its own state

    Most confusion about the pipeline comes from mixing these up. A person has a lifecycle stage. A deal has a stage and an outcome. A meeting has attendance. A booking delivery has a processing result. None of them copies another's state.

    Getting on the line

    Five ways a person comes in

    Every channel goes through the same identity function, resolveLeadIdentity, before any deal exists. What differs is the stage the person enters at and what else gets written.

    Identity

    Who is this? The rule every booking passes through

    Only strong identifiers decide who someone is: an email, a phone or a social handle the person really uses. Placeholder values never count, and nothing joins people by time window. On a Calendly booking the Instagram handle wins, because setters and DM closers often fill the form for the person they talk to.

    The booking pipeline

    A booking, from Calendly to the deal

    Calendly calls a webhook. The CRM stores the payload, files a delivery receipt, and processes it on a queue in one transaction. Every delivery ends in exactly one of four results, and only a blocked or failed one needs a person.

    Deal targeting

    Which deal a booking joins

    Once the person is known, the booking needs a deal. The handler asks these questions in order and stops at the first that answers. A booking never joins a deal that a missed meeting closed: the next booking after a no-show is a new deal, unless the closer set a next step.

    The deal

    The deal stage machine

    A deal is one attempt to sell one program to one person. It sits at one of five stages, and its outcome is open until it reaches Closed, where it is won or lost with a reason. Every move goes through transitionOpportunity and the stage machine in convex/opportunities/stages.ts.

    The meeting

    Meetings record attendance, nothing else

    A meeting is scheduled, held, no-show or canceled, each with its own recorded time. A no-show or a cancellation also closes the deal as lost with that reason, while the attendance stays on the meeting. A call still scheduled after it ends shows as Not recorded: a display rule, not a fifth state.

    The money

    Payment makes a customer

    Customer is a lifecycle stage, not a separate record. Recording a payment closes the deal as won, moves the person to Customer, and creates or updates an enrollment: one per person and program, derived from the payments.

    Credit

    Who gets credit on a deal

    Credit is a row: one team member, one role, one deal, with where the fact came from. Rows are only ever added; reassigning a meeting adds a row instead of replacing one. There are no commission shares.

    Time

    What runs on a clock

    Most of the pipeline reacts to events. These rules run on time instead, and they explain most surprises: a Slack deal that closed by itself, a webhook alert, a report that counts by the business day.

    When the rules can't decide

    Booking reviews and the recovery panel

    A booking that names more than one person opens a booking review, posted to Slack. A decision covers the people, not the one booking: a merge closes every review it leaves with one person, and keeping people separate closes every review about the same pair. Anything else that holds a booking waits in the recovery panel under Operations, where an admin retries it after fixing the cause.

    A worked case: one booking, two people

    The first booking held under the new rules shows every piece of this page at once. Names are left out.

    1. 9 OctLead gen captures an Instagram account from a reel. It becomes a person at Prospect, holding only the handle.
    2. 17 OctA second person books through an event type that doesn't map the Instagram question. Their record gets only their email, though the form carried the same handle.
    3. 19 Oct, 23:06 UTCA DM closer copies a booking link for the Instagram account. Five minutes later a booking arrives with the second person's name and email and the first person's handle.
    4. 19 Oct, 23:11 UTCThe email names one person and the handle another. The rule at the time holds any such booking, so its writes roll back and it waits in the recovery panel. Its cancellation the next morning is held too, because the booking it cancels was never recorded.
    5. 21–22 OctBooking reviews ship. Reviews open only when a delivery is processed, and this one isn't retried, so it never gets one.
    6. 23 OctAn admin merges the two records on the Leads page and retries both deliveries. The booking applies, its meeting is canceled, and its deal closes as lost with reason canceled.

    Two lessons. Every Calendly event type that books sales calls needs its Instagram and phone questions mapped, or the person's handle is dropped. And a rule change that opens reviews needs a one-off retry of bookings already held.
