Call insights from Fathom

Every matched Fathom call gets read by Claude for the prospect's objections, struggles, goals and income. Code checks every answer against the transcript, then Jev checks each item. Second opinion becomes part of it. Closers see it on their own calls, on the meeting screen and the person page.

The board above is a live simulation of the queue: each call is queued when Fathom's recording matches a meeting, read, checked, then saved or skipped with a reason. Names, quotes and example calls in this document are made up. Counts described as "in production" or "today" (payments, matches, no-shows, and the table of code measures in the no-show section) are real, with no names or quotes.

What it reads

Objections 12 kinds, plus whether the prospect let it go on the call
Struggles 11 kinds of business pain
Goals 11 kinds, with a target amount and deadline when said
Income every statement, its kind, currency and period

What it guarantees

Every item carries a quote that code finds in the transcript, said by the prospect, at a timestamp. If code can't find it, the item is thrown away.

An income amount must appear in its own quote. Currency is never guessed.

Then Jev rates each surviving item on the lines around it: confirmed, not sure, or rejected. Rejected items are hidden and go to the review queue.

Where it shows up

A Call insights card on each meeting (What they said + Second opinion), a From their calls card on the person page, one settings card, one review queue, re-run jobs, and a What prospects said section in Reports → Calls. On a meeting with no result yet, a suggested result (payment, call back, lost or no-show) with a button that opens the form filled in.

Owners and admins see everything. Closers see it on their own calls. Lead gen sees nothing new.

00 · Revised plan

What changed in v2

Your three changes, each worked into the design by the council (five reviewers, three votes), plus a fourth: suggested results that fill in the Won, Follow-up, Lost and No-show forms from what was said on the call. The rest of the first plan stays: quote checking, text-free facts, identity checks, the 90-day quote purge.

1 · Everyone sees it

Closers

Closers get Call insights on the meeting screen and the person page, for calls on meetings assigned to them, including income and quotes.

Unsure items show as "Not confirmed". Rejected items, Jev's scores and review state stay with admins.

Default: a closer sees a call's insights once they've recorded its result. One switch removes that (decision 1).

2 · Re-run older calls

Jobs

Pick calls that already have a Second opinion, were never read, or were read with an older version. See the count and the cost, set a ceiling, start.

Soonest-expiring transcripts go first. The old result stays on screen until the new one is saved. Reviews are never lost.

3 · Jev joins, Second opinion folds in

One feature

Call insights is the one name: one card, one settings card, one queue, one monthly limit.

Two parts: What they said (Claude reads, code checks, Jev checks each item) and Second opinion (Jev's check of the closer's result, unchanged, and it never sees Claude's answer).

Two parts, one feature

Second opinion is fenced off from Claude's output
01 · Architecture

The system in one picture

Call insights is a new Convex module that hangs off the Fathom ingest that already exists. Jev's Second opinion keeps its own code and table; it joins the shared budget, card and queue. The one rule that shapes everything: prospect words stay in Convex. Only categories and numbers travel to the warehouse.

System context

Prospect words: transcript, quotes, statements. Stay in Convex; leave only for the zero-retention model call. Text-free facts: categories, resolution, income numbers. Control: triggers, budget. Exists today New

The pieces, and why each one exists

Each part has one job, so each can be tested without the others.

03 · Reliability

    
Every run has a state, and every state has an exit

    
A run is one attempt to read one recording. It can't get stuck: a lease runs out, a sweep picks it up, a budget wait ends at the month's turn. Pick a scenario to watch the run move.

What the system writes

          
    
        
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    One retry owner. The Workpool's own retries are off. Our action decides: busy answers come back later (honouring Retry-After), everything else stops with a reason. No double charging, no loops.
    
      
    Claim tokens stop late workers. A run is claimed with a fresh token. If a worker that lost its lease answers late, publish sees the old token and refuses it.
    
      
    Budget never goes over. Every paid call (Claude, each Jev packet, each Second opinion check, each suggestion's early reading and result check) reserves the most it could cost from one monthly budget first. Two runs racing for the last dollar can't both win.

    A · A report reads half a sync
    
        
    
          
    Fivetran copies rows in batches. Right after a re-read, ClickHouse can hold the new header with only some of its items. Every item carries the header's publicationRevision; a call counts only when all its items of that revision are there.

    B · Two people get merged
    
        
    
          
    A call was checked against one person. If that person's emails change, the check may no longer hold (it might now be someone else's call). The header stamps who it checked and which version of their emails; a mismatch hides the call until it's re-checked.

Re-run jobs stop earlier, so Second opinion always has room.

      06 · Trust
      
    
      Claude proposes, code checks, Jev confirms
      
    
      The model returns items with a quote and a line number. Code checks each one against the transcript and drops anything it can't prove. Then Jev rates each surviving item on the lines around it: a quote can be real and still be filed under the wrong meaning. Rejected items are hidden and go to the review queue.
      

    
      
      
      Validation · one call, eleven proposed items
      Loops while on screen
      
      
      
        
      
        
      
          
      
            
      Proposed itemQuote foundProspect said itAmount in quoteProspect agreedJev confirmsResult
      
            
          
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Quotes are matched, not trusted. Code normalises case, accents and spacing, then looks for the quote in the cited line or one line either side. The timestamp and speaker come from the transcript, never from the model.
      
      
      Amounts must be in the words. "6k", "6,000", "seis mil" and "cinco lucas" all parse. If the number the model reports isn't in its own quote, the income item is dropped.
      
      
      Jev checks meaning. Code proves the words were said; Jev checks they mean what the item claims. It sees only the lines around the quote and one or two questions written by code. Confirmed items count in reports; "Not confirmed" items show on cards only.
      
      
      Rejected isn't deleted. An admin sees each rejected item in the queue. "Claude was right" puts it back on the card and in reports; "Jev was right" closes it. Both stay in the history.
      
      
      Speaker unclear? If two different non-closer voices are labelled Prospect, or nobody is labelled Closer, items are kept with a warning and that call's income stays out of reports.

      Nothing is recorded by itself. "Use this" only opens the existing form. Saving goes through the same mutations and side effects as today. Payments from a suggestion need proof for every role, admins included.
      
      
      Saves check the suggestion is still true. Each save carries the suggestion's revision. If a re-read changed it, the form shows the new values beside yours ("Use the new values" or "Save my values"). If the recording was moved, belongs to another prospect or expired, the suggestion is dropped and you save as normal.
      
      
      Second opinion stays honest. Its check doesn't change. Because a suggestion can now shape what the closer records, its agreement figure is split: results saved with no suggestion shown (the headline), shown but not used, used, used and edited.
      
      
      Prospect words stay private. Quotes and suggested values never go to ClickHouse, logs or PostHog. The card and forms are hidden from session replay; lines with card numbers read aloud are never shown or stored.
      
      
      Who sees it. Owners and admins on any meeting; the closer on their own meeting, before the result. Closers see values only when every check agrees. Mismatches after the result go to admins. Today's day-close Jev hint already shows a closer Jev's reading before the result; the suggestion shows it sooner, which widens rule §5.10 of docs/plans/closer-assistant.md.
      
      
      When it shows. Only after Fathom's recording arrives and is read, usually minutes after the call. Most payments are logged hours to days later on the meeting (of 291: 76 within 2 h, 94 in 2–24 h, 101 in 1–7 days), so the suggestion is there when the money arrives.

      08c · No-show
      
    
      No-show: measured by code, then read by two models
      
    
      No-show is a fifth kind of suggested result. It's the one the transcript can show almost by arithmetic: one speaker, the closer, and nothing where the prospect should be. Code measures that first, at no cost. Claude and Jev then read the call separately. When all three agree, the closer said they were leaving, and nothing contradicts it, the suggestion is Certain and one confirm records it. Anything less opens the no-show form for a person to review, or fills in nothing.
      

    
      
      
      Fathom can't see who joined, only who spoke. Its API has no attendee list and no join or leave events. Calendar invitees are who was invited, whether or not they came. A prospect who joined muted looks the same as one who never came, so silence alone is never enough.
      
      
      No recording is never a no-show. Fathom's own docs say a short call with too little audio may send no webhook at all. A missing recording, or one still on its way, gives no suggestion.
      
      
      Nothing records itself, yet. The transcript can't rule out a prospect who joined muted, or one who arrived after the recording stopped, and no recorded no-show has a recording to test on yet. So even Certain needs one confirm (decision 11). Today only Calendly's own no-show flag is recorded automatically, and it's reversed if Calendly withdraws it. A person's no-show can't be reversed; the deal can only be reopened as a follow-up.
      
    
      

    
      What the minute-by-minute record looks like
      
    
      Each square is one minute of the recording: closer, someone else, an outlined square when no line starts in that minute, and a dashed rule once the recording has stopped. The dashed line is the scheduled start; the bracket is the 10-minute wait.

      What code measures
      
    
      No model involved. Two rules shape it. First, count speakers by who they provably are, not by today's fallback label: when no speaker's email matches the prospect, today's code calls every speaker with no email who isn't recognised as the closer "Prospect", so the closer's second device ("Marco's iPhone") or a setter can count as the prospect. Second, use only what's stored: each transcript line has a start time and no end time, so word and line counts are exact while talk time is an estimate, and Certain never relies on an estimate.
      
    
      MeasureHowWhy it matters
      
      
      SpeakersOne key per speaker: their email, or their display name when Fathom gives no email. The closer's key is the recorder's email, or an exact match to their Magnus or Fathom name.       The gap no recording can close
      
    
      
      
      The prospect arrives after the recording stops. The waiting quotes are real and both models are right about the recording, yet the call was held. Nothing in the recording shows it. Fathom documents that a person can stop recording and stay in the call; it doesn't document whether coming back starts a second recording, so the plan doesn't count on one. Two things stand in for it: Certain needs the closer to say they're leaving at the very end, and a closer who was there confirms their own meetings. An admin confirming from the queue wasn't there, which is why the holdout below asks the closer.
      
      
      After a no-show is confirmed. The existing bar appears: create a reschedule link, or set a follow-up. From the admin queue, the admin can create the link, or leave it to the closer, who sees the no-show on their meeting. When the reason is late cancel or technical issues, the reschedule link comes first.
